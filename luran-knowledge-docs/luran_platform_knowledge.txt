Luran Platform Knowledge (Tenant Version)

What Luran is
Luran is a customer support platform that combines three support layers:
- AI answers from your uploaded knowledge base
- guided flows for repeatable support journeys
- human agent handoff for complex cases

Your team manages this from dashboard, while your customers interact through the website widget.

How customer support works in Luran
1. Customer opens your widget.
2. If you have active customer-facing flows, the customer can be guided through options.
3. If flow does not resolve the request, AI can continue with knowledge-based answers.
4. If needed, the conversation is escalated to a human agent.
5. Agent continues in dashboard with conversation context.

How AI answers are grounded
- You upload support content (FAQs, guides, policies, docs).
- Luran processes and indexes the content.
- AI retrieves relevant knowledge and uses it to answer customer questions.
- If knowledge is missing or weak, AI may ask clarifying questions or move toward escalation.

How flows and AI work together
- Flows are best for structured, repeatable journeys (for example support menus and troubleshooting paths).
- AI is best for open-ended questions that are not fully captured in fixed paths.
- Luran can use both in one customer journey: flow first, AI fallback, then human escalation when needed.

Typical flow use cases
- telecom-style support menu (Data / Airtime / Services)
- billing and payment troubleshooting
- onboarding and account setup help
- eligibility checks before escalation
- structured handoff to human support

Knowledge source lifecycle
- After upload, source enters `PROCESSING`.
- When successful, source becomes `ACTIVE`.
- If processing fails, source becomes `FAILED`.
- Deleting a source marks it `INACTIVE` and removes it from active retrieval.

When customers can expect good answers
- Best results happen when key knowledge is `ACTIVE`.
- If sources are still processing, answers may be incomplete.
- Keep high-frequency support topics updated for better response quality.

Escalation behavior
- Escalation can happen through flows or AI support logic.
- Customer can also request human support directly in conversation.
- When escalated, a support ticket can be created and agents can reply from dashboard.

Widget experience basics
- You embed one script snippet on your website.
- Widget uses your tenant settings (greeting, colors, logo, position).
- Your customer messages are linked to your tenant context.

Tenant isolation and data scope
- Your tenant data is separated from other businesses.
- Knowledge, chat sessions, flows, and support operations are scoped to your tenant.
- API keys are tenant-scoped.

API keys and safe usage
- Public key (`pk_live_...`) is for widget/frontend usage.
- Secret keys must not be exposed in browser code.
- Wrong tenantId/key combination causes widget config/auth errors.

Plan and usage enforcement
Runtime checks enforce plan capabilities such as:
- monthly conversations
- knowledge storage and source count
- team size
- flow limits
- webhook-related limits

If subscription state is inactive (for example trial expired), protected usage is blocked until reactivated.

Team operations in dashboard
Tenant admins can:
- manage knowledge sources
- configure widget settings
- build and publish flows
- monitor support activity
- manage users/agents and handle escalations

Best practices for strong support quality
- upload accurate, customer-facing documentation
- prioritize top customer questions first
- use flows for repetitive processes
- keep tone and wording consistent with your brand
- test end-to-end after major changes (knowledge, flows, widget settings)

Common reasons support quality drops
- outdated or incomplete knowledge content
- too many critical journeys left unstructured (no flow)
- invalid widget tenantId or public key
- plan limits reached
- inactive subscription state

Quick success checklist
1. Upload core FAQs/policies.
2. Confirm knowledge sources are `ACTIVE`.
3. Configure greeting and branding.
4. Add at least one customer-facing flow for frequent requests.
5. Test AI response, flow path, and escalation path before going live.
