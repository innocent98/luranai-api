Flows, Support Operations, and Team Workflow Guide (Tenant Version)

Purpose
This guide explains how to run structured support operations in Luran using flows, support queues, ticket lifecycle controls, and team roles.

Part A: Flows

What flows are for
Flows help you handle repeatable customer journeys with consistent steps.
Use flows when you want predictable outcomes, faster triage, and cleaner escalation.

Common flow use cases
- onboarding and setup help
- billing issue triage
- account access recovery paths
- service request qualification
- guided escalation preparation

Flow creation journey
1. Open Flows from the left sidebar.
2. Choose create flow (or start from a template).
3. Add and connect steps to reflect your support process.
4. Test key paths before activation.
5. Activate flow when ready for customer use.

Flow lifecycle states
- Draft: work in progress
- Active: available for customer handling
- Paused: temporarily inactive
- Archived: retired flow

Flow quality checklist before activation
- all customer choices are clear
- escalation branch exists where needed
- dead ends are removed
- language matches your support tone
- high-risk paths are reviewed by an admin

Plan-aware flow restrictions
Flow creation and usage are controlled by your current plan.
If you cannot create new flows, confirm:
- your current flow count vs plan limit
- subscription status
- role permissions

Part B: Support Operations

Support area overview
Open Support in the sidebar to access:
- Conversations
- Tickets

1) Conversations workflow
Use Conversations to review active/escalated customer sessions.

Typical process:
1. filter queue by status
2. open a conversation
3. take over if unassigned
4. continue customer support
5. resolve or transition to ticket if needed

Important behavior:
- agents can take over unassigned escalated conversations
- if a conversation is already assigned to another agent, takeover is restricted
- closed/resolved conversation states restrict further chat handling

2) Tickets workflow
Use Tickets for formal case tracking and resolution management.

Ticket lifecycle
- Open
- In Progress
- Resolved
- Closed

Typical process:
1. create ticket from escalated context
2. assign owner
3. work ticket to resolution
4. add resolution notes
5. close after confirmation

Priority levels
- low
- medium
- high
- urgent

Operational best practices
- always assign urgent tickets quickly
- require clear resolution notes
- close only after customer-impact checks
- link ticket handling back to knowledge/flow improvements

Part C: Team Roles and Collaboration

Tenant roles
- Tenant Admin
- Support Agent
- Viewer

What each role should focus on
Tenant Admin:
- controls team invitations and role management
- configures AI, widget, and key tenant settings
- oversees subscription/capability decisions

Support Agent:
- handles conversation takeover and customer support
- works assigned tickets
- records clean resolution outcomes

Viewer:
- observes operations and reporting
- no administrative management actions

Team invitation journey
1. Open Team Members.
2. Invite new member.
3. choose the appropriate role.
4. confirm seat/limit availability under current plan.

Plan-aware team restrictions
Team size is controlled by your plan.
If invitations fail, review current member limits and subscription status.

Part D: AI + Flow + Human handoff model

Recommended sequence
1. Let flows handle repeatable journeys first.
2. Let AI answer open-ended in-scope questions.
3. Escalate to human support for exceptions, risk cases, or unresolved issues.

This model gives:
- better consistency
- faster handling of common requests
- cleaner human focus on high-value cases

Part E: Navigation quick map (for operators)
- Build structured journeys: Flows
- Work escalated chat sessions: Support → Conversations
- Manage case lifecycle: Support → Tickets
- Invite/organize support staff: Team Members
- Tune assistant behavior: Settings → AI Configuration
- Manage customer-facing chat appearance: Widget
- Improve quality with metrics: Analytics

Part F: Continuous improvement loop
Weekly cycle:
1. review escalated conversations
2. review unresolved and reopened tickets
3. identify repeated causes
4. update knowledge and flow branches
5. adjust AI settings where needed

Monthly cycle:
1. review ratings trends
2. review flow performance
3. review support workload by queue and role
4. align team capacity with plan capabilities

Final principle
Strong support operations come from role clarity, predictable flow design, disciplined ticket handling, and regular updates to knowledge and AI guidance.
